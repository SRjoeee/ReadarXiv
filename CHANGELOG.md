# Changelog

Reader-facing changes, newest first. The design is `docs/DESIGN.md`.

## Unreleased

- Fixed: in the PDF reader, a table cell's translation is no longer drawn over the words of the cell beside it, which had left the cell's own place empty.
- In the PDF reader, the first translated page comes sooner on papers with large figures shown in two panes: the original's pages just below the view are drawn once the translation's first page is in place, so they no longer download beside it.
- The PDF reader's highlight no longer lights a whole figure or table. A caption lights sentence by sentence, as a paragraph does, and a table one cell at a time, its numbers too, in the same place on both sides. Pointing at a figure's picture or its labels lights nothing, since the original and the translation already stand side by side.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, a page where our copy of the paper sets a line or two on the page before or after it is now translated, as long as its text is the same. Before, such pages, and sometimes the whole first half of a paper, stayed in English.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, a translation that holds a symbol such as &, % or # no longer shows a backslash before it ("CAD\&CG" now reads "CAD&CG").
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, when the pages you are looking at are ones the reader shows as the original (its own typesetting of the paper does not match arXiv's there), the progress line ends when the translation does. It had stayed on until a translated page further on was drawn.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, a paragraph that ends in a footnote mark, such as an abstract with a note on its last sentence, is translated; it had been left in the original language, whole.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, a bibliography made with biblatex follows the references switch like any other: each entry is translated only when you turn the references on, and its heading is translated on its own, no longer run into the first entry.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, a caption or paragraph with several bold or italic labels ("Left:", "Center:", "Right:") keeps every label in that style in the translation. Before, only the first stayed bold when Microsoft translated it.
- The PDF reader now translates into Italian, Dutch, Polish, Turkish, Indonesian, Malay, Czech, Swedish, Ukrainian and Bulgarian. Each language uses its own words for figures, tables and headings, and breaks words at the end of a line by its own rules.
- Malay in Latin letters is now a target language. Until now, Malay was offered only in Jawi script.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, with the references turned on, the page numbers some papers print after each reference (the pages that cite it, each a link back) now follow the reference's translation, still links, instead of staying where the English reference was and showing over the translated text.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, a heading a paper sets against the edge of its column inside a box, such as "Abstract" over a boxed abstract, keeps that edge in the translation, where it had been drawn about a character in.
- In the PDF reader, French, Spanish and Portuguese words are hyphenated at the end of a line by the rules of their own language, where English rules had been used.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, an underscore in a translated paragraph, caption or table cell ("task_spec.md", in a paper set in LaTeX's default fonts) and the line under underlined words no longer stay behind as stray dashes under the translation. In a list of figures, the full stop after a formula at the end of an entry is translated with the entry, no longer left beside it.
- In the PDF reader, the labels of a figure made with matplotlib are measured by their own letters, so a full stop no longer counts as tall as a capital when a translated label looks for room beside the figure's lines.
- In the PDF reader, the spaces in a Chinese, Japanese or Korean translation are drawn in the reader's own typeface, so they are as wide on every device.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, more of a paper is translated. A paragraph whose citations are small raised numbers set after the full stop or comma (as optics and physics journals print them) is translated: 19 paragraphs of one paper had stayed in English. A table that ends its rows with a shorthand of the paper's own is translated cell by cell, where 30 cells of one paper had run into the next row. A theorem, a paragraph or a table cell that holds a label, a colour or a note the paper prints as nothing is translated. A small drawing set inside a sentence is no longer sent to the translator. With the references turned on, an entry that ends with its DOI or its arXiv number in the style of the astronomy, Springer and physics journals, an entry whose initials are written in APA style ("Harrington, J. E."), an entry whose web address holds a percent sign ("%3F"), and each entry of a reference list that also holds a bare web page are translated, each as an entry of its own: about 270 entries of 15 papers had stayed in English or been run together.
- In the PDF reader, the reading options and the settings page have **What to translate**: text in figures, text in tables, headers and footers (shown translated unless you turn them off), and the references and the author names (shown as the paper prints them unless you turn them on). Each switch shows or hides that part's translation at once, without translating anything again. The references switch covers each entry whole, the names in it included; the author names switch covers only the names under the title, and the affiliations under them are always translated. The image translation switch now applies to HTML pages alone, and is greyed in the popup while the PDF reader is open; the PDF reader's text in figures starts as you had that switch.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, the references of a paper set in REVTeX (the template of most physics journals) are each an entry of the references, shown translated only when you turn the references on, like any paper's; they had been run together, in translation, into paragraphs as long as a column, the original's blue links standing in the middle of them. In a paper whose author block centres each line on its own (ICLR 2021's template), the authors' names stay as the paper prints them, and the notes and the affiliation under them are translated each in its own style, no longer bold with the names.
- In the PDF reader, the lines of a translated paragraph stand evenly apart. Where the original opened a little extra room around a tall formula, that room no longer stays under whichever translated line lands there: it goes with the formula, which keeps the room the original gave it. A paragraph, heading or quotation the paper sets ragged on the right keeps its ragged edge in the translation, where it had been stretched to both edges, and one the paper sets against the right margin stays there, where it had moved to the left. An abstract a journal's style sets in a box of its own (such as Elsevier's) keeps its own alignment, no longer that of the paragraph after it.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, a Chinese or Japanese dash written as two em dashes (——) is never split across two lines, and a word too long for its line is never cut between a letter and its accent. Letters of scripts that are not Chinese, Japanese or Korean (Latin and Cyrillic extensions, a paper's own symbols) are drawn in the Latin typeface, no longer in the Chinese one. A translation whose answer holds a malformed character code no longer stops the paper's translation, which retrying had repeated. The formulas of a paper set in Euler or MathTime fonts stay as the paper prints them, and a figure's label, a running head or a note set in XITS's text face is translated. The words of a figure a paper draws in TikZ are sent to the translator once, no longer twice.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, the labels of a figure's legend or axis are drawn at one size, the original's wherever they fit, each starting where its original started (or ending where it ended, for labels set against an axis). A translation longer than its label now takes the free space beside it, never over the figure's lines or past the figure's edge; only when one label of a group still does not fit does the whole group shrink, together. In a legend, one entry had been drawn much smaller than the others, and a shorter translation had been centred where its column starts on the left. A figure's label whose Chinese, Japanese or Korean translation keeps a Latin word or a number ("MLP", "2", "RGB") is translated too: it had stayed in English.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, an entry of a paper's references whose year carries a letter ("2020a") or that ends with its DOI is translated, also in a paper that draws a small figure inside a table. Such a figure had kept the PDF reader from learning what those entries print, and 8 entries of one paper had stayed in English. In a paper whose text uses many macros of its own, the references' entries are now laid out for translation too: 54 entries of one paper had stayed in English.
- In the PDF reader, the translation is drawn as dark as the page around it. On a Mac its letters had come out about a fifth lighter than the original's text and than other tools' translations in the same typeface; papers set in Computer Modern or Garamond (most LaTeX papers) were lighter still, since Chinese, Japanese and Korean took a light weight beside them, and now take the same regular and bold weights as beside every other typeface. Where the browser would place the letters even a fraction of a pixel differently by drawing them this way (Chrome on Linux), it keeps drawing them as before.
- In the PDF reader, the affiliations, institutions, addresses and e-mail line under a paper's authors are translated, each where the paper sets it; the authors' names stay as the paper prints them. An affiliation a paper writes once in a macro of its own is translated too.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, a paragraph in which a paper's own shorthand that adds its space by itself (such as "SNR" or a tool's name) comes right before a formula, a citation or a footnote mark is translated: 24 paragraphs in 12 of 32 papers, an abstract among them, had stayed in English. An entry of the references that ends with its DOI ("doi:10.…") is laid out for translation too: 56 entries in 3 papers had been left as the original.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, the text a paper's class prints by itself (a theorem's head whose note is only a citation, such as "Definition 1 ([TT])", a journal's "AMS subject classifications.", a bold heading over a table) is no longer drawn over the original's words, which had stayed visible under the translation. It now keeps the bold, italic and colour of its own words, where it had been drawn all in one style. The closing bracket and full stop after a theorem's note that ends in a citation now follow the note's translation, where they had stayed at the original's place.
- In the PDF reader, laying out a translated page takes about a quarter less of the browser's time on a slow device, so scrolling stays smoother while pages are translated.
- In the PDF reader, the words inside a paper's drawn figures are translated: axis titles, legends, a plot's title, the labels of a diagram's boxes and arrows, in a figure the paper draws in TeX or includes as a PDF. Each is drawn where the original's was, in its colour, along its line (a y-axis title stays turned), shrunk a little where the translation is longer and left as it was where it would not fit, overlap the figure's lines, or where the figure's own fonts give no readable text. Text in figures, under What to translate in the reading options, shows or hides them at once, without translating anything again. Numbers, a single letter and formulas in a figure stay as they are.
- In the PDF reader, the text a paper's class prints by itself is translated too: the table of contents and the lists of figures and tables (each entry in the words of the heading or caption it repeats, its number, dots and page number as the paper prints them), the running heads and feet, notes no paragraph holds, and the paragraphs of a title page or a thesis's front matter. A running head that repeats the title or a chapter's name reads as that title's translation, the same on every page. Authors' names and a paper's references stay as the paper prints them unless you turn them on under What to translate.
- In the PDF reader, a page still being translated shows the original with one soft light crossing its paragraphs, until the whole page is drawn in translation at once (a still tint where your system asks for less motion).
- In the PDF reader, the names a paper gives its own theorem-like statements ("Assumption 2", "Conjecture 1", "Claim 3") and the names in the references it generates that no TeX package translates into the language you read (in Chinese, "Section A.7" and the "and" of "Sections 5.2.1 and 5.2.4"; in Japanese, Korean and Traditional Chinese, every theorem's name) are translated too, once a paper, and the same wherever they appear.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, the head of a theorem, a definition, a lemma and the like ("Definition 5.1", "Proposition 5.1"), a proof's "Proof." and the names in a reference the paper generates ("Theorem A.17", "Figure 3", a citation's "Lemma 1") are translated, in the names the TeX packages give each language; the numbers stay as the paper prints them. A reference that has no such name in the language you read (in Chinese, "Section A.7") stays as the paper prints it, never half translated. A paragraph that begins with a reference ("Theorem 5.1 below shows …") no longer shows that reference twice.
- In the PDF reader, the pages you have just read come back translated at once when you scroll back to them, never the original or an empty page first: as many as the device's memory allows, the farthest from where you read let go first. While you rest on a page, the next two are prepared, so that they are translated when you reach them. The fonts a page is drawn in come in one go instead of one paragraph after another, so the first translated page appears sooner.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, a theorem's or lemma's title set over two lines ("Lemma A.2 (Functional characterization … [Bobkov and Ledoux, 2009b, Proposition 6.1])."), whose translation fits on one, stays on its first line beside "Lemma A.2 (". It had moved down onto the second line and was drawn over the first words of the lemma.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, a heading or caption the paper sets in small capitals (the ICLR, ICML and IEEE templates do) is translated at its own size, no longer smaller than the text around it, and in German, Spanish, French, Portuguese and Russian it is drawn in small capitals at the proportions of the original's, the same in every browser. A word in small capitals inside a sentence, and a Latin word inside a Chinese, Japanese or Korean translation, is drawn in small capitals too. A heading the paper sets in capitals (the physics journals' REVTeX template does) is translated in capitals.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, a table cell or a paragraph that opens with a number such as "1,024", a word in code type or a short formula, and the title of a paper in ICML's style, are translated again: 12 in 4 papers had stayed in English.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, paragraphs that had stayed in English since the release candidate are translated again (18 in 25 papers): one with an author's e-mail note at the foot of its column, a footnote that runs on to the next page or ends with a formula, a word with a letter such as ø or ł, a funding note, a heading run into its paragraph, a title that the paper repeats in its reference block, and a paragraph whose formula, table or web address contains words of the text that follows it. The closing bracket and full stop of a theorem's note, and the full stop after a heading run into its paragraph, no longer stay under the translation.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, the items of a list set inside a paragraph ("(1) … (2) …") no longer draw over each other: an item's translation ends on its last line where the original's did, before the next item's number. A centred caption numbered for a supplement or an appendix ("Table S3:", "Figure A1") stays centred with its number. A paper that comes with a supplement as a second main file is translated again (one paper of fifty had stayed in English).
- Fixed: a translation service whose model does not answer in the shape the extension asks for (prose instead of JSON, say) no longer turns one batch of four paragraphs into 40 paid requests: it is asked once more, then the next service on the chain takes over, two requests in all. The free web translators are spared the same way.
- The PDF reader and the control panel do nothing when a page that is not the extension's own puts them in a frame or sends its tab to them: no translation is started, no setting is changed, and a click there acts on no tab. The floating button answers only your own clicks, never a page's script.
- On the settings page, an API key you type is sent only to the address of the service you are adding: the model list no longer loads by itself from an address you had not chosen for the service on the way to typing yours (Read arXiv's website among them). A request that carries your key never follows a redirect to another address.
- When Microsoft's or Google's free translator refuses a request, the popup and the PDF reader say the service refused access, no longer that an API key is invalid: these services use none.
- In the PDF reader, when Read arXiv's website is at its limit for you, the reader says there are too many requests and to try again later, no longer that the bilingual PDF can't be loaded. It asks again by itself three times at most, then waits for you to press Retry; before, a tab left open asked every ten minutes for as long as it stayed open. A paper whose preparation was refused for the moment is asked for again after the wait, no longer taken for one that cannot be read as a bilingual PDF.
- A translation can no longer wait for ever on Chrome's built-in translator: if Chrome does not say within two seconds whether it can translate, the extension goes on with the next service.
- Translations stored under an earlier version of the extension's cache are deleted once they expire, where they had stayed until the cache was full.

## 0.5.2 — 2026-10-22

- Fixed: in the PDF reader, a sentence whose words change style (bold, italic, colour) is translated whole. It used to come back in pieces, in one paper Chinese words separated by spaces, and a short label such as "Before" could vanish from the translation.
- The translation keeps the original's colour, bold and italic, read from the original's own letters: a coloured word keeps its colour, a citation or a reference keeps its link colour with its brackets black (`[16]`, "Figure 1"), and a label set in bold before plain text (`Batch size: 16, 32`) no longer turns the whole line bold.
- A heading centred on the original page stays centred in the translation, and a heading that runs into its paragraph stays on the same line. A centred caption on a page of figures stays on one line, and the brackets and full stop that close a theorem's note are drawn again.
- In Chinese and Japanese, no stray spaces appear between characters, and a space the author sets by hand (a thin space, an em space) is drawn at the width the author set. Italic in Chinese, Japanese and Korean is drawn slanted, by 13 degrees.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, a piece of code or a web address inside a sentence is drawn from the original's own letters. It used to leave the whole paragraph in English when the original set that piece over two lines. A straight quote, a backslash, a caret or a vertical bar in a paper's text no longer leaves its paragraph in English either: in one paper, 256 paragraphs.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, code and command output that a paper sets in a block style of its own are never sent to the translator; they stay as the paper prints them. One paper had 832 such blocks, and their punctuation had been turned full-width.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, a paper that puts a box of text straight before a centred passage can be prepared, and the words after a box are translated whole. The first word after a box could lose its first letter, and a figure's internal name could be sent for translation.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, a paragraph whose reference arXiv's PDF names otherwise than the copy prepared here ("appendix B" for "section B") is translated, also where the other name moves the paragraph's lines. Two paragraphs of one paper stayed in English.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, a paper whose prepared copy stops short of arXiv's pages, or had been broken in preparing it, is no longer drawn from that copy: it is mended and translated like any other (three papers of fifty had stayed almost wholly in English). A paper that comes with several main files, such as a paper and its supplement, is translated on the first file's pages, the supplement's pages left as the author wrote them.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, more papers can be prepared: those in some physics journals' style with numbered appendices (six of nine such papers could not be), and papers that use some less common ways of laying out their figures and contents.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, the paragraphs of papers whose files were written on Windows no longer run together: in one paper an introduction of ten paragraphs and a proof of sixteen were each translated as one block, and text could move onto the page before. Each paragraph is now translated and placed on its own.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, a formula set on a line of its own that ends its sentence with a full stop or a comma is read with that sentence, so the translator no longer runs the next sentence into it.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, the lines before a displayed formula or a forced line break are no longer stretched to the column's edge, which spread Chinese characters apart; and a formula that was broken over lines is drawn whole, as the author set it, instead of with its subscripts and superscripts lost.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, each sentence of a paragraph that goes on over a page break is drawn on the page its source is on. A paragraph that goes on over a page is translated where a formula's row at the next page's head, or a footnote going on over the same page break, used to keep it in English; a paragraph and a footnote that both go on over the same break no longer take each other's lines. Translated text is no longer drawn over a table that text wraps around.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, pointing at a sentence of a paragraph with a displayed formula lights that sentence and its translation, formula included. On the translated side a sentence could light only up to the first formula on its page, or nothing. Where a sentence cannot be told apart from its neighbours, the paragraph lights whole on both sides, never another sentence. A paragraph that runs on to the next page lights by sentence from the first time you point at it.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, translated pages no longer show faint traces of the original's letters at some zoom levels, among them the usual fit on a laptop's screen. The translation and its links now sit exactly over the page at every zoom.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, the fraction bars, root bars and overlines of older arXiv papers no longer stay under the translation (one paper had 56), and their title bar shows the paper's title instead of arXiv's margin stamp.

## 0.5.0 — 2026-10-19

- PDF papers have a reader of their own: arXiv's PDF on one side, its translation on the other, page for page. Figures and formulas stay as the author drew them, the translated text is set in fonts close to the original's, and the two sides scroll together; point at a sentence and its counterpart lights up.
- Links work in the translated pages. A citation or a reference jumps to its place in the paper, a web address opens in a new tab, and every link can be reached with the keyboard; Enter on a citation goes to the bibliography and the focus goes with you. References written as "Figure 1" or "sections 3.2 and 3.3" are links too.
- On a large paper, the first translated page no longer waits for the whole PDF to arrive. Jumping far into a long paper shows the page you land on sooner.
- The first time anyone opens a paper, the reader says it is being prepared and shows its progress; a paper that cannot be prepared right now says so, with Retry and a link to its HTML version.
- The files of the PDF papers you read are kept on your computer, up to 200 MB, the papers opened longest ago going first, so that a paper you reopen opens sooner and still opens when Read arXiv's website cannot be reached. The settings page shows how many are kept and clears them. Only the paper's identifier and version go to Read arXiv's website: no text of a paper, no translation and no key; the translation itself is made, as for an HTML paper, by the service you chose.
- The reader's Download opens arXiv's own PDF in a tab of its own, in the browser's viewer.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, a translated line after a short last line no longer runs over the formula set on a line of its own below it.
- Fixed: when arXiv or Read arXiv's website takes a request and then goes silent, the PDF reader and the floating button no longer wait for ever: the reader ends in its failure note with Retry, and the button appears.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, text printed at an angle (a caption up a landscape page, a figure's label turned on its side) is highlighted where it is printed. One paper with landscape pages could not be opened at all.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, the section headings some papers write with a plain-text version for the PDF's bookmarks are translated. They stayed in English: 13 of one paper's 14 headings.
- Fixed: in the PDF reader, a set or a tuple that a paper writes as several formulas side by side — `{Φ0, Φ1, Φ2}` written as three, its brace opened in the first and closed in the last — stays whole in the translation. The translator could move one of its parts away, and the set was drawn broken apart around the translated words.
- Fixed: the permission to reach a service's address is given back once no service uses it, every time. A service deleted while the settings page closed before its five seconds of Undo were up kept that permission, and pages translating with it went on using it until they reloaded; now they move to the next service and the permission goes. An address you edited away, or asked for in a form you then closed, goes back too — never while a page still translates with it or a form is connecting to it.
- In the PDF reader, an address that names no paper now says so — "The paper can't be found", with a link to arXiv — instead of loading for ever.
- If the extension's settings do not answer within a second and a half, the PDF reader, the popup and the settings page appear anyway. In the reader the paper opens on the defaults, a note says the settings could not be read, and the controls that change them are greyed until they can be; a change the browser refuses to store says "Couldn't save. Try again." until you close the note or a later change goes through. The reader's language and service menus keep their places in the bar while the settings load, so it no longer shifts.
- In the PDF reader, closing the "only part of this translation can be shown" note no longer hides the count of passages that failed. The page arrows, a note's close and the contents' folds show tooltips; screen readers find the paper's title as the page's heading at every width, and the arXiv link says what it opens. On the settings page, Escape turns a "Clear now" confirmation back.
- In the PDF reader's Side by side display, scrolling while the paper is still opening no longer leaves the two sides apart: once both are ready, the other side comes level with the one you scrolled. It used to stay where it was, up to several pages off, until your next scroll ended.
- In the PDF reader, the message at the foot of the page moves as one: a longer message waits for its capsule to open, a shorter one changes first and the capsule closes after it, and a count changes in place, digit by digit. A language the bilingual PDF does not support yet has its name drawn in that language's own letterforms.
- A new look for the popup, the settings page and the floating button, drawn from the PDF reader's: the same greys, type, controls and motion, and a new red — arXiv's own, to the eye — for the one main action on each screen and for the switches of the popup and the settings page (the PDF reader keeps its own). The popup's name and its main button are larger. Every row of the popup and the settings page is measured so that its parts stand on one line.
- One appearance for the whole extension — follow the system, light or dark — set on the settings page, under Appearance, or in the PDF reader's reading options. The reader used to have its own and the popup and the settings page followed the system; what you had chosen in the reader is now the extension's.
- The popup finds papers. On a page that is not an arXiv paper, type or paste into its search field: a paper's PDF or HTML link opens its translation, an abstract link, a paper's id or its arXiv DOI offers the HTML and PDF translations, and anything else searches arXiv in a new tab when you press Enter.
- The popup's menus open under their rows, and each list you can change ends with a way to manage it — services, prompts, styles — that opens the settings page at that place. A paused page offers "Retranslate" and "Show original" side by side. On an abstract page or a PDF the popup shows the two ways in, HTML and PDF, with the service and the language, and nothing only a translated page uses.
- The settings page, rebuilt: four sections — Translation, Appearance, Reading, Data — a search that finds a setting by its name or what it does, and links that open a setting in place. The page sits in the middle of a wide window. The interface language sits at the foot of the sidebar, beside a globe, and at the end of the title's row on a narrow window.
- A service you add is added once it connects. Enter its address (OpenRouter, DeepSeek and a local Ollama are one click away), its key and its model — picked from the service's own list when it offers one — and Connect. A service that cannot translate a sentence is no longer saved. Editing one works the same way, in place.
- A key the service refuses is remembered. The popup, the settings page and the PDF reader say "API key no longer valid" for that service; with the automatic switch on, pages go to a free service instead of trying the refused key again; and connecting with a new key — or the same one, once your account is fixed — clears it.
- Deleting a service, a prompt or a style is undone rather than confirmed: its row says it was deleted, with Undo, for five seconds. Clearing a cache, which cannot be undone, asks once more in place.
- The glossary is a table, one term a row; paste lines of "source, translation" to add several at once. The translation styles are a list, each written in its own style, and every one — built in or yours — is edited in place; "Restore built-in styles" brings the shipped ones back. The hover highlight's colours are swatches, one of them yours to pick.
- Two ways to translate: As you read, the default — the paragraphs you reach and those just below — and Whole paper. If you had chosen two or three screens ahead, or to start only when half or all of a paragraph shows, you now have As you read.
- The words in figures show in every display — split, stacked and translation only — whenever figure translation is on; the per-display ticks are gone. If you had unticked every display, figure translation stays off.
- The floating button and the figure viewer take the new look. The button follows the extension's appearance; the viewer's controls and a failed paragraph's retry follow the paper's own theme on arXiv.
- The PDF reader's highlight follows the sentence you read: point at one and it lights on both sides, the original and its translation, from the moment the first pages of the translation appear. A paragraph whose sentences cannot be matched on both sides lights whole on both; headings, captions and table cells always light whole.
- In the PDF reader, tables, algorithms and figures light whole with their captions, on both sides: a table to its rules, a figure outlined so its colours stay true. Pointing at a panel of a figure lights it with its own caption, and its main caption lights them all.
- The PDF reader's highlight covers what it lights cleanly: one shape over a paragraph's formulas, inline or set on lines of their own, nothing lit beside it and no gap inside it, from a theorem's or a list's label to the paragraph's last full stop, and a sentence that runs on to the next column or page reaches across to it.
- A sentence with an abbreviation in it — "Oregon v. Mitchell", "Mt.", "Ch. 3", "Eqn. 5", "Sect. 2" and the like — is no longer taken for two, and one that ends on a formula is no longer joined to the next: the hover highlight covers each sentence, and with Google or your own service each is translated as one.
- In the PDF reader, every page is named for screen readers by its side and its number, in the interface's language — "Original · Page 3", "Translation · Page 3" — where PDF.js named each one "Page 3" in English, in both panes. In a narrow window, the arXiv id no longer shows for two frames before it leaves the toolbar.
- On the Read arXiv website the extension says it is installed and which version it is, so the site can skip its install offer: one attribute on the page, nothing read from the page and no message sent. It runs on the website alone, and adds that address to the pages the extension may read, which Chrome may ask you to accept after the update.
- Fixed, with Microsoft Translator: the last word of a sentence that ends in a footnote mark or a citation after its full stop, as physics papers set them, came back in English.
- Names in figures stay as the paper writes them. With Microsoft's or Google's translator, or the one built into Chrome, a figure's labels are translated one at a time, and a name standing alone — a model, a benchmark, a method — came back as ordinary words: `HellaSwag` became "hell's battle" in Chinese, `Claude Opus 4.8` "Claude's works 4.8". ## 0.4.0 — 2026-09-17

The first release. Numbered 0.4.0 rather than 1.0.0 (the owner, 2026-09-17): the number continues the line of the MVP's tags (`v0.1.0-phase1`, `v0.2.0-phase2`, `v0.3.0-mvp`), and small numbers suit a project that still changes often — fixes go to 0.4.x, features to 0.5.0. Read arXiv translates arXiv's HTML papers in place and keeps them working.

### Reading

- Three modes — split, stacked, translation only — switched at any time; restoring the original is lossless (the page is node for node what it was).
- Formulas, citations, cross-references, code, links, footnotes and tables survive translation; a footnote appears once, in the margin, original above translation; figures split into both columns in side mode.
- Sentence highlight on hover, on both sides at once — every paired block lights up, sentence by sentence where the service reports boundaries and as a whole where it does not; in translation-only mode the original sentence floats beside the translation. In-page links to a hidden original land on its translation.
- Long papers stay smooth: the highlight follows the pointer and translations land without the page pausing — no style rule makes the browser re-examine the whole document any more (a 160 ms pause per sentence on a 60 000-element paper before).
- The translation's appearance is yours: colour, opacity, underline, blur-until-hover, advanced CSS; background highlights likewise; built-ins are ordinary entries you can change or reset.
- Translate from the popup, the toolbar button, the right-click menu, Alt+T, or the **Bilingual version** link on a paper's abstract page.
- Translation follows your reading: paragraphs one to three screens ahead are requested as you scroll, or the whole paper at once if you prefer; a paragraph never scrolled to costs nothing.

### Services

- Microsoft's web translator by default, needing no account; Google's web translator and Chrome's built-in offline translator as well; any OpenAI-compatible endpoint you add (OpenRouter, DeepSeek, a local Ollama or LM Studio) with its own key and model.
- A failing service — an expired key, a spent quota, a dropped connection — hands the rest of the page to a free one and the popup says so; turn the fallback off to stay with one service.
- Prompts (built-in, copied and edited, imported and exported) and a glossary for the LLM services; a paper's title and abstract go with every request as context.
- 179 target languages; a service that cannot serve the chosen one says so before you start. Interface in English and Chinese.

### Images

- The words inside figures are translated and overlaid in place: vector figures and TikZ pictures without any install, bitmap figures on macOS through a small local helper (Apple Vision) installed with one command from the popup; the popup notices when the install is done.

### Privacy and data

- Paragraphs go to the service you chose; Chrome's built-in translation and a local endpoint keep them on your machine. API keys stay in the browser's extension storage and never enter logs, the cache or the diagnostics export.
- Translations are cached locally (30 days) so a paper reopens at once; the settings page shows the cache and clears it.
- Saved settings this version cannot read — written by a newer version, or damaged — are left exactly as they are: the defaults are used, the settings page says why, and nothing overwrites what was saved until you choose to reset it.
- A diagnostics log — request failures, hand-overs, the extension's own trace lines, never page text or a key — can be exported from the settings page to attach to an issue.

### Compatibility

- Chrome 131 or newer (Chrome's built-in translator needs 138; on 131–137 it is simply not offered).
- The macOS helper is installed at `~/Library/Application Support/Readarxiv/helper` and registered as the Native Messaging host `io.github.srjoeee.arxivtranslate`; the install command is stamped with the extension's own version, so the two match.
